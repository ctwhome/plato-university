The theme of the year —

Thinking in the
Age of the Machine.

What does it mean to have a mind, now that minds can be bought by the hour?

Every Plato University cohort is organized around a single question that runs through the whole year. The founding cohort asks the one we think the decade will demand an answer to.

i.
Trimester one

What is a mind?

Plato, Descartes, Ryle, Turing, Searle, Dennett. The oldest question, read cleanly, in the order it actually developed. We do not skip to the good part.

ii.
Trimester two

What is a machine?

From Babbage's engine to the transformer. Not a survey — a close reading of the four papers and two books that explain why our moment exists, and what it owes to the ones before it.

iii.
Trimester three

What is a life, now that the two can be confused?

The practical trimester. Every student ships a project defending an answer — an essay, a tool, a company, a piece of writing, a syllabus. Their capstone.

The twelve questions

Each seminar answers one.

i.Can a machine think? What would it take to know?
ii.Is the mind a kind of computation, or only sometimes?
iii.What is judgment, and can it be automated?
iv.Is language a tool we built, or a house we live in?
v.What does it mean to know something, versus to retrieve it?
vi.Who owns a thought produced with a machine's help?
vii.What is a good life when most labor becomes optional?
viii.Is education the transfer of skills, or the formation of a person?
ix.What does a city owe its young — and what do the young owe the city?
x.Should we trust a mind we did not raise?
xi.What is the honest work of a human being in 2036?
xii.Having read, argued, and built — what will you defend?

The shape of the year

Four months each. One theme throughout.

Students
Thirty — max.
Seminar-sized. If the room doesn't fit a conversation, the number is wrong.
Seminars
One hundred and forty-four.
Twelve per month, twelve months. One question per seminar.
Mentors
One per student.
A working adult, in your city, whose job is not to grade you.
Capstone
One defended publicly.
Thirty minutes of questions in front of the room. Pass or not.
The theme is not a slogan. It is the only question on the syllabus. — Reading notes, seminar one.
