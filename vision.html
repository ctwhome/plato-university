The vision · 2026 — 2036

We are still running a school built for the factory floor.
We're building the one that comes after.

Plato University is a one-year academy for the AI era. Philosophy first. Portfolio last. It is small, city-rooted, unapologetically literary, and — for the right kind of mind — the most honest schooling available in the decade ahead.

A letter from the founder Amsterdam · Spring 2026

The modern university was built to produce a very particular kind of person: a reliable employee for the industrial economy. Rows, bells, grades, majors, and the Carnegie Unit — these aren't timeless features of education. They're nineteenth-century inventions, designed for a nineteenth-century problem.

That problem has been solved. The economy it was built for is already dissolving. What replaces it is not a new syllabus. It is a different shape.

We think the replacement looks less like the research university and more like Plato's Academy — a small community of people reading primary sources, asking hard questions, defending their work in front of one another, and walking home through a city they know by name.

The thesis, in four movements

i.

AI has made "how to do the job" the cheap part.

Within a decade, almost every technical skill that carries a premium today will be a commodity. What remains rare — and worth paying for — is the capacity to judge what is worth doing, to argue for it, and to make it well. That is not a technical skill. That is a philosophical one.

ii.

The answer is not more job-training. It is less.

Bootcamps, nanodegrees, and micro-credentials all double down on the premise the industrial university got wrong — that the point of school is employability. That premise was always fragile. AI just made it untenable.

iii.

Cities are the campus.

A university does not need a quad. It needs a room with chairs arranged in a circle, a seminar that meets weekly, and a city that can hold both. Amsterdam is hub one. Every city with five or more students becomes its own hub. This is not online school. This is online reading, in-person thinking.

iv.

We graduate thinkers with a portfolio and a defended capstone.

Not a diploma. Not a transcript. A body of work and a half-hour of hard questions survived in front of a room of mentors. We think this is a better credential than the one our institutions still sell. In five years we expect the market to agree.

Learn to think before you learn to work. The tagline, and the whole program in seven words.

What we are against · what we are for

We are against credentialism, debt, early specialization, and the Prussian bell. We are for the examined life, made portable.

The industrial university has an enemy: itself, eighty years on. Ours has an enemy too — the quiet assumption that a young person should spend a decade of their life buying the right to be employable. We reject it. We think you can build a better life, and a better body of work, in one year of serious reading and serious making, inside the city you already live in.

That is the whole bet.

— with conviction, the founding team Plato University · Amsterdam
