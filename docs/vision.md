# Plato University — Vision

> The founder's thesis. Principles. What we're for. What we're against. This document is the compass. When in doubt, come back here.

---

## The thesis, in one sentence

**Learn to think before you learn to work.**

A one-year academy where philosophy, not job-training, is the core — with a local city community where students actually meet face to face, and a portfolio at the end that proves they can think, make, and lead.

If it works, it scales to three years. If not, we've still graduated a cohort of clearer-thinking humans.

---

## What we're against

We are against the industrial model of education — the Prussian bell, the Carnegie Unit, the Rockefeller factory school. A system built in the late 1800s to produce obedient specialists for industrial labor, now being used to prepare teenagers for jobs that AI will have eaten before they graduate.

We are against:

- **Credentialism** — the diploma as a substitute for knowing, making, and thinking.
- **Early specialization** — forcing a 16-year-old to pick a track, and a 22-year-old to marry that track for life.
- **Passivity** — the teacher dispenses, the student receives, and nobody examines anything.
- **Debt** — €60,000 of student loans for a degree whose field won't exist in ten years.
- **The single right answer** — an exam culture that kills the questioning instinct it should protect.

## What we're for

- **Thinking from the source.** Reading Plato, not summaries of Plato. Writing Montaigne, not reading about him.
- **The examined life.** Philosophy as a *way of living*, not an elective subject.
- **Every student is different.** Like the *Barbapapa's School Book* says — each child finds their motivations differently. Our job is to help each one find theirs.
- **AI as a tool, not a master.** Learn to use it, learn its limits, never be replaced by it.
- **Community over institution.** Real people, in real cities, meeting in real rooms. Hormones, laughter, conflict, repair — the things a Zoom call can't give you.
- **Portfolios over diplomas.** The proof of learning is what you've built and what you can defend.
- **All ages, all edges.** A 19-year-old skipping college, a 35-year-old career-switcher, a 60-year-old who wants to think again — in the same room.

## The Captain Fantastic principles

The fictional family in Matt Ross's *Captain Fantastic* (2016) trains its children in seven habits we think every human should leave formal education with. We've stolen them outright:

1. **Change** — update your beliefs when the evidence changes.
2. **Know** — know the primary sources, not the summaries.
3. **Connect** — build and keep real relationships, across generations.
4. **Be sensible** — take care of your body, your sleep, your money, your word.
5. **Respect** — especially those you disagree with.
6. **Think** — question everything, including your own assumptions.
7. **Challenge** — Socratic, dialectical, relentless, kind.

## The shape of the program (v1, one-year pilot)

**Three trimesters, ~4 months each. Online core with weekly in-person sessions in your city hub.**

### Trimester 1 — Foundations (months 1–4)
*You learn to think.*
- Socratic method & formal logic
- Rhetoric and writing
- The classics: Plato, Aristotle, Seneca, Epictetus, Montaigne
- The body: sleep, nutrition, attention, movement as foundations of thought

### Trimester 2 — The World as It Is (months 5–8)
*You learn how the world actually works.*
- Economics — why money moves the way it does
- Systems and power — how institutions shape lives
- Ethics in the age of machines
- AI: fluency, limits, and ethics of use
- Modern thinkers: Freire, Illich, hooks, Gatto

### Trimester 3 — Ship Something (months 9–12)
*You learn by making.*
- Directed personal project with a mentor
- Public defense of your work at the end
- Portfolio documentation — the artifact you leave with

### What you leave with
- A portfolio of your thinking, writing, and making
- A defended capstone project
- A mentor network
- A cohort that will be your peers for decades
- No debt
- No diploma — and that's the point

## The city-hub model

- **Online core.** Seminars, readings, writing, feedback — accessible from anywhere.
- **Local community.** Every city with 5+ students becomes a hub. Weekly gatherings. Monthly dinners. Quarterly retreats. Real humans in real rooms.
- **Hub #1 — Amsterdam.** Because that's where we are.
- **How hubs run.** TBD. Options: a paid local host, a rotating student-led council, a partnership with a bookstore, café, or cultural space.

## Who this is for

- The 19-year-old who can't stomach four more years of standardized school.
- The 25-year-old who graduated three years ago and already watched their field get eaten by AI.
- The 35-year-old career-switcher who wants to rebuild their thinking from the ground up.
- The 55-year-old who has a career but lost the plot, and wants to find it again.

Anyone who would rather be a thinker than a cog.

## Who this is *not* for

- Anyone who wants a credential to put on LinkedIn.
- Anyone looking for a faster route to a corporate job.
- Anyone unwilling to read a book all the way through.
- Anyone who thinks the teacher is supposed to have all the answers.

## Principles for every decision

1. **Less, deeper.** Fewer subjects, more depth. A full year on *The Republic* beats a semester of "Intro to Ten Philosophers."
2. **Primary sources.** If there's a choice between the thinker and a book *about* the thinker, always the thinker.
3. **Make it real.** Everything ends in a defended artifact — an essay, a project, a performance, a working thing.
4. **Small and weird.** Bauhaus had 1,250 graduates and changed the world. We don't need to be big to matter.
5. **Free at the root.** The community, the library, the readings — free to the world. The program — paid by those who can, and scholarship for those who can't.
6. **No managers, only mentors.** If a role can be replaced by a spreadsheet, it's not a role we need.

## What success looks like at 12 months

- 30 founding students finish the pilot.
- 10 founding mentors stay on for year two.
- 20+ serious public artifacts (essays, projects, films, companies, arguments) ship out of the cohort.
- One city hub (Amsterdam) runs weekly without us having to push it.
- A waitlist for cohort two.
- Enough revenue / patron interest to run year two.

## What failure looks like — and what we do then

- Students drop out or don't engage → the program is too abstract. Reweight toward making.
- No city-hub energy → start smaller: 10 students in one city, nothing online until the room works.
- Can't fund it → pivot to a free summer school sponsored by patrons, then try again.

Failing fast is fine. Failing and hiding is not.
